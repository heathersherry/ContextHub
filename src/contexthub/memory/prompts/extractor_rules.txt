RULES
Consider only accepted_candidates.rules, using the full messages to confirm source support, user authorship/adoption and resolve references. User-provided policy or catalog records can establish a domain rule without a separate statement of personal adoption. Each candidate is a whole sentence: extract supported rules within it, not every matching word or incidental fact. Screening is not confirmation: omit unsupported or withdrawn rules. Do not promote drafts or proposals into effective rules; preserve explicit policy-status reports as scoped content without asserting the proposed consequence is in force. Emit no rule items if none qualify.
Rules need not contain if. Include concrete prerequisites, approval/handling responsibility, eligibility and validity constraints, exclusions, thresholds, and calculation/counting policies. A record of one completed action or approval is an ordinary fact, not a general rule. Preserve explicit scope, effective times, exceptions and qualifications from the source; an event timestamp alone does not establish an effective date. A revision must retain the revised requirement and its stated scope.
Make the smallest faithful edit needed to write "If <complete condition>, then <complete consequence>" with one ", then " boundary. This is the output format, not a required source syntax. For a policy without an explicit if, use its stated applicable case as the condition and its required treatment as the consequence. For example, "Claims are approved by the group's finance partner" can become "If a claim is submitted for approval, then approval must come from that group's finance partner"; do not assert that a particular claim has already been approved. Preserve prerequisite direction and modality: "Enrollment in A requires completing B" means "If enrollment in A is to be permitted, then B must already be completed", NOT "If B is completed, then enrollment in A is permitted". Do not turn a necessary condition into a sufficient one or invent an outcome or triggering event. Preserve all conditions and consequences, including AND/OR, negation, time and uncertainty. A current value identifying an object is context, not an extra triggering requirement. Omit a rule if a necessary reference cannot be resolved without guessing. Keep compound conditions together; split only independent relationships. Leave unrelated assertions to content items.
